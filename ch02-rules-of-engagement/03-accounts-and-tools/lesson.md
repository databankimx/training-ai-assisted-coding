# Accounts and Tools

Chapter 0 covered setting up your account. This step is about the habit that keeps you inside the rules: checking, every time, which account you're using and whether the tool is allowed for the work in front of you.

The mistake is rarely deliberate. A personal account is still signed in on the machine, the company tool opens in a browser tab where you've been using a different login, or a free tier is the only one you've used before. The fix is to check, and to make the check automatic.

## Which account, for which work

The policy says two things. Use only AI tools approved by IT. And do not use personal accounts for company-related work. Put those together and you get a simple table.

| What you're doing | Which account |
|---|---|
| Work for DataBank, including code, documents, and questions about company systems | A company-provided account, through an approved tool |
| Learning on invented or sample data, as in this course | The free tier of GitHub Copilot is fine, as Chapter 0 explained |
| Anything you're unsure about | Ask first, and use the company account until you know |

Paid GitHub Copilot seats are assigned to developer roles. Everyone else can use any company-provided AI tool, with manager approval.

## The check

Before you start a session with an AI tool for company work, look at three things.

1. **The account name** shown in the editor or the tool's settings. If it isn't the company account, sign out and sign in with the right one.
2. **The browser tab's profile**, if the tool runs in a browser. Many people have a personal and a work profile in the same browser, and the tool can remember the wrong one.
3. **The tool's own settings**, for any option that shares your prompts with the provider for training. Company accounts are covered by the company's agreements. A personal account is not.

If any of these is wrong, stop and fix it before you paste anything.

## How a new tool gets approved

Suppose you find a tool that would help with your work. The policy's process is:

1. **Don't use it yet.** The policy says not to use any new AI tool or service until it has been approved by IT. A sandbox approved by IT is the only place to test one before approval.
2. **Tell your manager.** Access to approved tools is granted by managers, so your manager is part of the first step.
3. **Open a helpdesk ticket.** The ticket starts the review. For a new vendor, the policy requires a formal vendor risk assessment before you proceed.
4. **Wait for the answer.** Until you have it, the tool is not approved for work. A project that needs it is delayed, and that is the correct result.

Expect the process to take time. It's designed to take time, because the review is the protection.

## Try it

Check your own setup against the three-point check above, and write down what you find in a file called `account-check.txt` in your practice folder. Then imagine you've found a new AI writing tool that a colleague recommends. Write the message you'd send your manager to start the approval process, in about three sentences. Keep it short, and don't include any company data in the message.
