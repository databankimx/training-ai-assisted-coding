# Choosing the Right Surface

Most people meet an AI assistant in one place, and they use it for everything. A chat window becomes the place where they ask questions, draft code, plan a feature, and fix a bug, usually in one long conversation. That works some of the time. It works badly when the task calls for a different way of working.

This chapter is about the ways you can work with an assistant, which this course calls surfaces, and about choosing one before you write a prompt. A good prompt sent through the wrong surface still gives a poor result.

## Why the surface matters

Each surface gives the assistant a different view of your work and a different amount of control. An inline suggestion sees the lines around your cursor. A chat sees whatever you paste into it. An agent can read files, run commands, and make several changes in a row. The more the assistant can see and do, the more it can get wrong without you noticing, and the more care the task needs.

Choosing the surface is a decision about how much you trust the output, how much you need to check it, and how much room you're willing to give the assistant. It's the same decision a senior developer makes before delegating work to a junior one.
