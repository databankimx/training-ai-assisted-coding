# Rules of Engagement

Chapter 0 introduced the company's AI policy, and Chapter 1 used sample data throughout. This chapter takes the rules out of the summary and into the work you actually do. You'll sort real-looking items, decide what to do with the cases the policy doesn't settle for you, and learn what to do when something goes wrong.

Some of this will repeat what Chapter 0 said. That's deliberate. A rule you have only read once is easy to forget on the day you need it, so this chapter comes back to the most important points from new directions.

## Why the rules come first

It's tempting to treat policy as a box to tick before the real work begins. In practice, the rules decide what you can paste into a prompt, which account you sign in with, and whether a suggestion you're about to accept can go into a client's system. Get those decisions wrong and the code quality doesn't matter, because the code can't be used.

The company's position is simple to state. Anyone can do development, but everyone follows the rules. This chapter is about the rules, and it assumes you're going to follow them.

## What you'll do

The chapter has five steps before the homework and the summary:

1. **Classifying what you have.** You sort a set of items into allowed, not allowed, or ask first.
2. **Grey areas.** You work through cases the policy doesn't settle directly, and learn a method for deciding them.
3. **Accounts and tools.** You check which account you're using, and learn how a new tool gets approved.
4. **Code that leaves the building.** You learn the development rules that apply to anything delivered to a client.
5. **When something goes wrong.** You learn what to report, to whom, and why reporting quickly matters more than getting it perfect.

The homework is a sorting exercise, with an answer key behind a spoiler.

## Sources

The policy itself is on the employee portal, and Chapter 0, step 5 links to it. The development rules of engagement are in this repository, in `development-rules-of-engagement.md`, and they apply to any code you write for a client. When this chapter quotes or summarizes a rule, check the source before you rely on it. The policy and the rules change, and this course is a guide to them, not a replacement.

If you're not sure whether a rule applies to your situation, ask your manager or open a helpdesk ticket. The rules tell you to ask in that case, and they count a question asked in good time as the correct response.
