# When Something Goes Wrong

You will eventually paste something you shouldn't have, or accept a suggestion that causes a problem. Everyone who uses these tools does. The policy is written with that in mind. What matters is what you do in the next few minutes, and the rule is to report it promptly.

The policy says to report any issue or incident involving AI immediately, to IT and Information Security, by opening a helpdesk ticket or calling IT support. The issues it names include a security breach, a policy violation, and an AI output that is incorrect or biased. A suspected breach is also reported to your supervisor, the CIO, the CTO, or Human Resources.

## What counts as an incident

- Sensitive data went into an AI tool that isn't approved, or into an approved tool on the wrong account.
- A credential, key, or token was pasted anywhere outside the company's systems.
- An AI tool produced output that caused, or nearly caused, a security problem, a wrong decision about a customer, or a biased result.
- Code generated with an AI tool was delivered, or was about to be, without the review and scans the rules require.
- You're not sure whether any of the above happened. Report it anyway, and let the people who investigate decide.

## What to do, in order

1. **Stop.** Don't paste anything more into the tool, and don't try to fix the problem by sending more data to find out what happened.
2. **Note what happened.** Write down what you shared, where, when, and with which account. Your memory will be less accurate later, and the investigation will need these details.
3. **Report it.** Open a helpdesk ticket, or call IT support if the situation is urgent. Tell your manager too. For a suspected breach, the policy also names your supervisor, the CIO, the CTO, or Human Resources.
4. **Don't delete evidence.** Leave the chat history, the files, and the account as they are, unless the people investigating tell you otherwise. Deleting something to make the problem go away can make the investigation harder.
5. **Cooperate.** The policy says AI-related incidents are formally investigated, and that employees are expected to cooperate fully.

## Why speed matters more than perfection

It's natural to hesitate. You might think you can fix it first, or that it isn't serious enough to report. The policy doesn't ask you to judge that alone. A report that turns out to be nothing costs a few minutes of someone's time. A report that comes late can cost the company a breach notification, a client relationship, or the chance to contain the problem while it's still small.

Reporting promptly is the behavior the policy asks for, and it's the behavior that protects you as well. Stopping to report is never the wrong move.

## Try it

Write a short incident note for a made-up situation in a file called `incident-note.txt` in your practice folder. Imagine that you pasted a customer's invoice total and an account number into a chat window with a personal account, and you realized it a few minutes later. Include what you'd record in step 2 above, and the first sentence of the message you'd send to the helpdesk. Keep the note factual, with no guesses about blame.
